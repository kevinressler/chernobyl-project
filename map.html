Exhibit 02

Radiation Map

How the fallout from Reactor No. 4 spread, from the plant's early days, across Europe in the spring of 1986, to the zone as it stands today. Use the timeline or press play.

About this map. Plume paths and contamination areas are simplified illustrations based on published reconstructions, not measured data. Contamination is shown as caesium-137 deposited on the ground (kBq/m²). It fades on the map as the caesium decays, with a half-life of about 30 years. The timeline stops are events, not evenly spaced years.

Sources: UNSCEAR 2008 Report, Annex D (health effects of the Chernobyl accident) and its 2018 white paper on thyroid cancer; IAEA, Chernobyl's Legacy (Chernobyl Forum, 2006); European Commission, Atlas of Caesium Deposition on Europe after the Chernobyl Accident (1998); IAEA updates on Chornobyl (2022, 2025).
